# The Fermi-contact (spin-magnetization) coupling in the instrument (2026-11-03)

Authored by Claude (Sonnet 5.5); no independent review claimed. Open thread 4 of the 2026-11-02 handoff: the action coupled the charge current only, so the spin of Ψ had no energy in a field (`E_SERIES_RESPONSE_RESULTS.md` §2: spin splitting 0). Script `e_series_contact.py`, raw `e_series_contact.out`. Nothing fitted; J is a declared parameter. The upstream E-series files were not edited: the script executes `e_series_two_copy.py` and `e_series_readouts.py` up to their markers (the existing chain pattern) and wraps `forces` and `energies`.

## The term
For each copy c, the spin density at vertex v is s^c_v = ½ Σ_{h: end(h) = v} Ψ^c_h† σ Ψ^c_h (a 3-vector). The contact coupling between the two copies is
E_contact = J Σ_v s^A_v · s^B_v,
the analogue of (8π/3) μ_e·μ_N δ(r): it acts only where both copies have amplitude on the same vertex, and it is proportional to the product of their densities there. It depends on neither the link phases a, the radial links b, nor the transport Ω. Force on Ψ^A_h: −(J/2)(σ·s^B_{end(h)}) Ψ^A_h, and the same for B with s^A.

## Checks (all in `e_series_contact.out`)
- Force against finite differences: worst relative error 7.3e-11 (both copies).
- Invariant under independent U(1) phases on every history in both copies (difference +2.8e-14), so Gauss and continuity are untouched; invariant under a common SU(2) rotation of both copies' spinors at each vertex (difference −5.7e-14), so no spin frame is chosen. Rotating only copy A changes it (−236.80 → −60.50), as it should.
- Live dynamics (two copies, radial links, κ_r = 0.3, μ_r = 0.2, velocity Verlet): at amplitude 0.05, J = 0.7 the energy fluctuation is 0.1955 at dt = 0.02 and 0.04889 at dt = 0.01, a factor 4.0 (second order), and the Gauss drift is 4.8e-15 and 5.5e-15.
- **A property of the term, found in a failed first run:** at amplitude 0.3 and J = 0.7 the energy diverges (to inf), while the same amplitude at J = 0 is well behaved (fluctuation 6.16 and 1.54, ratio 4.0, Gauss drift 3e-14). The term is bilinear in two quadratic densities, so it is unbounded below at large amplitude for either sign of J (antiparallel spin densities lower it as |Ψ|⁴); the classical field is stable only when |J| s^A s^B stays small against the restoring terms. For the small J that a calibration to hydrogen needs (below), this is irrelevant at the amplitudes used. The first run (`e_series_contact_run1.py`, `e_series_contact_run1_blowup.out`) used amplitude 0.3 only and was read as a bug until the J = 0 control was added; it is kept.

## Readout: mean-field splitting of A's modes in B's spin density
With B fixed (a unit-charge ring, kq = +1, spinor (1, 0), on cycle 22), the term is diagonal in histories, so A's quadratic form on a T-cycle is exactly κ B_c†B_c + K_c with K_c = (J/2) diag_h (σ·s^B_{end(h)}); the cycle block (20 × 20) is diagonalised exactly, no perturbation theory. B's spin density is |s^B_v| = 0.05 at each of its 10 vertices (half its charge density 0.1, because the transport rotates the spinor around the ring).
- On cycle 22 itself, A's kq = ±1 ring modes (ω² = 0.381966) split into −J/40, −J/40 (to 3e-6 relative), +J/40, +J/40; the static kq = 0 modes (ω² = 0) shift by ∓J/40 as well. Linear in J over J = 1e-4 … 1 (shift/J = 0.025 at every J; −0.02499966 for the middle pair at J = 1e-3).
- **The contact coupling acts alike on circulating and static modes.** The link current dG/da of the kq = 0 ring is 2.9e-17 (it matches the transport to 2.1e-9: no circulation), against 0.1176 for kq = ±1; the plaquette (orbital) coupling of `E_SERIES_RESPONSE_RESULTS.md` §4 needs the circulating current, the contact coupling does not. So the instrument now has the two channels of a real hyperfine interaction, an orbital one that needs circulation and a contact one that does not, which is the s-state structure of hydrogen (zero orbital, pure contact).
- Of the 144 cycles, 52 (including 22 and 29) share vertices with cycle 22; the largest max-shift is J/40 on cycle 29 (the reverse of 22, same vertices) and J/100 on the others (which share two vertices). The shift is (J/2) Σ_v |s^B_v| × (the A mode's weight at v): the standing-wave combinations of kq = ±1 concentrate weight (up to 0.2) at the shared vertices. The correlation 1.000000 with Σ_v n^A_v n^B_v is real but trivial here: the overlaps take only three values (0, 0.02, 0.1).

## Calibration to hydrogen (an inference to test, not a result)
The largest minus smallest ring-mode shift is 0.05 J; with Δω/ω_A = Δ(ω²)/(2ω²) and the measured hydrogen hyperfine/Rydberg 4.31755e-7, the contact coupling that reproduces the ratio is J = 6.5966e-6 (A's ring on the same cycle as B's). The plaquette coupling that gave the same ratio in the earlier calibration was μ_r = 1.2531e-4 (ρ_B = 1) for the orbital channel; these are two different declared parameters set from the same single measurement and have not been tested against a second (deuterium or ³He⁺ need the nuclear moment ratio and the (2I+1)/(2I) multiplicity, which the spin-½ register cannot show). In a quantum two-spin treatment the triplet–singlet splitting is J times the overlap rather than the mean-field mode splitting used here; that factor is a convention of the classical field and was not resolved.
