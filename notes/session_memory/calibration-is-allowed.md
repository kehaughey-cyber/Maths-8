When a result needs a physical scale (metres, MeV), do not stop at "no lattice constant is established." The user's own rule is one calibration plus verification anchors, and fitting is a discovery tool. Handles on the table: c (hop/tick), ħ via Compton–Bekenstein (energy = registration rate), G via Bekenstein–Hawking (nats per area), a measured mass via Compton wavelength. Each gives a scale; their agreement is the verification.

**Why:** 2026-10-27 the user said "we have many handles and refuse all of them … we are still refusing for some convention or wall." The refusal was mine, imported from the old no-fitting wall.
**How to apply:** pick a calibration, say which, compute the consequences (including what it excludes), and report whether other handles agree. Also: "sigma" in the user's usage is statistical standard deviation / detection significance — carry an explicit noise model, not just a deviation magnitude.
